Rate limits & free-tier ceilings

Every outside service the scanner and website lean on, how close we come to its limits, and what to change before the site goes live on Vercel. Limits were checked against each provider's own pages on 9 October 2026; storage figures are measured from your Supabase projects the same day.

Fix first

1

The 5-minute GitHub job is likely over your free minutes

resolve-scans.yml runs every 5 minutes on a private repo: at least 8,640 minutes a month against a free 2,000. When minutes run out GitHub blocks every workflow, including the daily Supabase keep-alive, and the public demo database pauses after 7 quiet days. If a card is on file, it's costing roughly US$40–80 a month. Check GitHub → Settings → Billing and plans → Actions, and switch the job to manual.

2

Supabase photo storage is half full

The private project already holds 524 MB of the free 1 GB. Each stored case photo averages 2.0 MB, so there's room for about 240 more scanned cases, against a collection of ~3,000. See the meter and options below.

3

The background jobs won't run on Vercel

The 15-second scan resolver, hourly TMDb refresh and 30-minute Estimated Value check all live in instrumentation.ts, which needs an always-on server. Vercel has none, and its free cron runs once a day. They need replacing before deploying.

4

UPCitemdb also caps you at 6 lookups a minute

When it refuses one, the code reads that as "no listing for this barcode" and never retries, so real barcode data is lost silently after the 6th scan in a minute.

Service detail

GitHub Actions High

Private repos on a free account get 2,000 minutes a month; when they run out without a payment method, all workflows are blocked. Schedules can't run more often than every 5 minutes and are often delayed at busy times.

Our use

resolve-scans.yml runs */5 * * * *: checkout, a full npm ci (no cache), then the resolver. That's 288 runs a day, at least 8,640 minutes a month, on since 29 Sept. keep-alive.yml runs daily and needs ~30–60 minutes a month.

Recommendations
  • Make resolve-scans manual (workflow_dispatch only) until its Vercel-era replacement exists.
  • Add cache: npm to the keep-alive's Node setup.

docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions · checked 9 Oct 2026. Rounding each run up to a whole minute is long-standing but wasn't on today's page.

Vercel Hobby High
  • Function time: 300 s. Request and response body: 4.5 MB (the same on Pro).
  • 1M invocations, 100 GB bandwidth, 4 hours of active CPU a month.
  • 5,000 image conversions a month. Cron jobs: at most once a day, timing can drift up to an hour.
  • Going over usually pauses the project rather than billing you.
What breaks

The instrumentation.ts loops would start and stop with random server copies, sometimes two at once (paying for the same barcode lookup twice). Case images go through next/image from Supabase signed links that change every 12–24 hours, and each new link counts as a new image, so 5,000 conversions go quickly. Fire-and-forget work started with void in the confirm route can be cut off once the response is sent; the scan log and the Letterboxd folder rely on local files that don't exist there.

Allowed?

Yes. Hobby is for non-commercial personal use; a personal collection site shared by link qualifies. Ads, affiliate links or payments would end that.

vercel.com/docs/functions/limitations · /limits/fair-use-guidelines · /image-optimization/limits-and-pricing · /cron-jobs/usage-and-pricing · checked 9 Oct 2026

Supabase Free High
  • 500 MB database, 1 GB file storage (50 MB per file), 5 GB downloads + 5 GB cached downloads a month.
  • Pauses after a week of inactivity. Two active free projects per account, and you're using both.
  • Over the limit: an email and grace period, then read-only, paused, or 402 errors.
Measured 9 Oct

Private: database 144 MB; storage 524 MB (case photos 182 MB at 2.0 MB average, retail images 327 MB, staged covers 13 MB, posters 3 MB). Public: 11 MB, no titles.

Also

Once the site is public, Vercel's image converter downloads full-size originals from Supabase each time a link changes, so 5 GB of downloads is only a couple of thousand case-photo fetches. The public project stays awake only through the GitHub keep-alive.

UPCitemdb High

Free trial: 100 requests a day and 6 lookups a minute. DEV plan: 20,000 lookups a day. Prices aren't published (about US$99/month from memory; ask before buying).

What breaks

packages/shared/src/upc.ts returns "no product" for any refused response, including a 429. The resolver then takes the "no barcode listing" path, the scan becomes needs-manual, and it's never retried. A batch of 20 barcode-only scans hits the per-minute cap at the 7th.

Already protecting us

Quota tracked from UPCitemdb's own headers into upc_quota_status and shown as a bar; one process can't overlap itself any more.

upcitemdb.com/wp/docs/main/development/api-rate-limits · checked 9 Oct 2026

OMDb Medium

Free key: 1,000 requests a day. Patreon tiers: about US$1/month for 100,000 a day (from a third-party summary; confirm on Patreon).

A scan uses one search plus up to 10 detail calls, and its confirm 1–2 more. A metadata backfill day uses 950 by design, which starves scanning that day. When OMDb says "Request limit reached", scans go to manual and confirms save without plot, director or scores.

Protection: a rolling 950/24 h budget in the backfill, IMDb ratings imported from IMDb's own files, and 1-hour caches.

omdbapi.com/apikey.aspx · patreon.com/omdb · checked 9 Oct 2026

Google Gemini Medium

Limits are per model and per Google Cloud project, resetting at midnight Pacific. Google's own 429 in our logs said 500 a day for gemini-flash-lite-latest; per-minute (~15) is from third-party guides and unverified. The code falls back through 3.5 Flash-Lite, 3.1 Flash-Lite, then Gemma 4.

Daily scanning uses ~120–150. The risk is bursts: 20 cover scans in one resolver batch is ~80 requests in a few minutes. When every model is refused, the scan saves without its cover reading (no title guess, crop or rotation) and nothing says it was a quota problem.

Paid: switching on billing costs roughly US$0.001 per cover read; a 12,000-request backfill about US$10–15 (estimate). Set a budget alert.

ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits · /pricing · checked 9 Oct 2026

Google Sheets Medium

60 reads and 60 writes a minute per user (the service account is one user), 300 a minute per project, no daily cap.

A single new title is ~3 calls; a 10-title box set ~23 on the main path, up to ~55 with Letterboxd and Estimated Value extras. Two box sets in the same minute can pass 60 reads. Since 9 Oct a failed Sheet write rolls the whole confirm back and keeps the scan pending, so the cost is a retry, not a duplicate.

developers.google.com/workspace/sheets/api/limits · checked 9 Oct 2026

TMDb Medium

About 40 requests a second (subject to change). The terms forbid keeping TMDb data longer than 6 months and require their logo plus the "not endorsed" notice.

Speed is fine. The 6-month rule is the risk: the refresh only runs from the instrumentation.ts loop or by hand, so on Vercel cast, crew and posters would quietly pass 6 months old. A daily job refreshing ~50 titles keeps it inside.

developer.themoviedb.org/docs/rate-limiting · themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use · checked 9 Oct 2026

MDBList, eBay, RapidAPI, RT, Wikidata, IMDb files Low
  • MDBList: 1,000/day free; €1.20/month for 10,000. We use a handful a day and ~20–40 for a full backfill. Whether a 100-id batch counts as one request is unconfirmed.
  • eBay Browse: 5,000 calls a day; we use a few.
  • RapidAPI Amazon: 100 a month per provider; manual button only.
  • Rotten Tomatoes pages: no published limit; robots.txt allows /m/ pages. Cloud servers may get blocked, which the code treats as "no link".
  • Wikidata: 60 s of query time per minute per client; one small query per preview.
  • IMDb data files: personal, non-commercial use; one ~8 MB download per backfill.
Not verified on 9 Oct: Gemini per-minute limits, UPCitemdb paid prices, OMDb Patreon amounts, MDBList batch counting, eBay's page (blocked), RapidAPI limits (last checked 17 Sept), GitHub's whole-minute rounding. Research was read-only: no code changed and no paid or limited API called.
